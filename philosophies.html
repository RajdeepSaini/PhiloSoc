BRANCH 01 · INNER FORTRESS

STOICISM

"You have power over your mind — not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."

"The happiness of your life depends upon the quality of your thoughts."

— Marcus Aurelius · Meditations
MARCUS AURELIUS SENECA EPICTETUS ZENO OF CITIUM
MARCUS AURELIUS · EST. 161 CE
CORE PRACTICES 01 / 04
01

The Dichotomy of Control

Divide all reality into what is within your volition (judgments, desires, moral character) and what lies outside it. Suffer only for what you author.

02

Amor Fati — Love of Fate

Do not merely tolerate adversity; actively desire it as the raw material for virtue. What stands in the way of the action becomes the action.

03

Memento Mori & Clarity

Constant mindfulness of mortality strips away trivial vanity, petty grievances, and paralysis — leaving only present integrity.

EMERGES FROM RIGHT →
BRANCH 02 · RADICAL FREEDOM

EXISTENTIALISM

"Existence precedes essence — man first exists, encounters himself, surges up into the world, and defines himself afterwards."

"Man is condemned to be free; because once thrown into the world, he is responsible for everything he does."

— Jean-Paul Sartre · Being and Nothingness
SARTRE NIETZSCHE KIERKEGAARD DE BEAUVOIR
THE UNWRITTEN SELF · PARIS 1943
CORE CONCEPTS 02 / 04
01

Radical Freedom & Vertigo

There is no predetermined blueprint, divine destiny, or biological excuse. With every action, you forge human nature in your image.

02

Bad Faith (Mauvaise Foi)

The quiet cowardice of pretending you have no choice — hiding behind institutional roles, societal scripts, or fatalism to avoid responsibility.

03

Authenticity & Self-Authorship

Living authentically requires confronting anxiety (Angst) and courageously choosing your own values in a universe without external validation.

BRANCH 03 · THE REBEL'S LAUGHTER

ABSURDISM

"The absurd is born of this confrontation between the human call and the unreasonable silence of the world."

"There is only one really serious philosophical problem, and that is suicide. Deciding whether or not life is worth living."

— Albert Camus · The Myth of Sisyphus (1942)
ALBERT CAMUS THOMAS NAGEL FRANZ KAFKA
THE MYTH OF SISYPHUS · THE ENDLESS ROCK
THE SISYPHEAN TRIUMPH 03 / 04
"One must imagine Sisyphus happy."
WHY MUST SISYPHUS BE HAPPY?

Condemned by the gods to roll an immense boulder up a mountain for eternity, only to watch it roll back down every single time. His punishment is the ultimate metaphor for human existence: repetitive, exhausting, and completely devoid of objective purpose.

Why happy? In the precise pause when Sisyphus descends back down the mountain to retrieve the stone, he is completely conscious of his fate. He harbors no delusions of divine rescue and no cowardly hope for a heavenly reward. By consciously embracing his futile struggle with defiance and joy, he renders the gods completely powerless. His fate belongs to him; his rock is his thing. The struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a human heart.

BRANCH 04 · SUSPENSION OF JUDGMENT

SKEPTICISM

"I do not define anything; I suspend judgment, and I remain inquiring."

"Que sais-je? (What do I know?) — The only thing that is certain is that nothing is certain."

— Michel de Montaigne · Essays
PYRRHO SEXTUS EMPIRICUS DAVID HUME MONTAIGNE
EPOCHÉ · THE EQUIPOISE OF EVIDENCE
CORE PRINCIPLES 04 / 04
01

Epoché (Suspension of Judgment)

When conflicting arguments possess equal persuasive weight, withhold consent. Refuse to adopt unprovable dogmas simply to appease cognitive discomfort.

02

Ataraxia (Untroubled Tranquility)

Pyrrho observed that our suffering arises not from reality itself, but from the violent defense of beliefs we can never decisively verify. Let go of dogmatism to find peace.

03

Radical Empirical Doubt

David Hume dismantled the certainty of causality: we never perceive cause and effect, only constant conjunction. Doubt is the foundation of genuine scientific integrity.
